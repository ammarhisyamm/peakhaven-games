# PH Arcade
<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

Web; mobile first with a centered 480px content column on larger screens.

## Users

Peak Haven customers aged 30–50, approximately 65% female per the user.

## Product Purpose

Nine casual arcade games with short, satisfying loops: Vial Tower, Vial Merge, Lab Sort, Peak Blocks, Bottle Mahjong, Orbit, Ridge Run, Bond and Sequence. English UI, device-local progress and no customer account. Lab Sort, Bottle Mahjong and Sequence have sequential puzzle levels. Vial Tower, Vial Merge, Peak Blocks, Orbit, Ridge Run and Bond are endless runs with saved personal bests and increasing challenge during play.

## Capabilities and Constraints

Coins and gems are local in-game currency earned through play and free daily rewards. Six collectible bottle skins equip in Tower. A daily free spin, daily merge challenge and multi-game coin boost support return visits. Profile shows actual XP, statistics, bottle ownership and achievements. The retry flow spends prototype coins and presents its payment as a simulation; it is not a real transaction. There is no payment, cash or store value and no cloud-save claim. Existing customer Site audience stays private.

Existing version-one browser saves and personal bests remain compatible. Sort, Mahjong and Sequence store their own unlocked puzzle level; winning unlocks the next board and failure retains the current level. The level console initially presents 18 or more nodes with Show more levels; this is a view window rather than an 18-mission cap. Prior completed mission IDs remain save data, while the Arcade menu exposes three puzzle ladders and six endless modes. Legacy mission links route to Arcade.

All nine games preserve their real mechanics, tutorial, help/pause, results and coin retry flow. The established puzzle tools remain available in the games that support them. Blocks has an 8×8 grid and three shape slots; Mahjong is a layered open-pair board; Sort changes live numbered liquids; Tower stacks proportional vials; Merge collides and combines matching tiers inside a glass chamber. Orbit launches bottles into a spinning ring; Ridge Run taps a vial through rising glass gates; Bond pins bottles to a rotating ring; Sequence swaps crystals on a 7×7 match-three board with a 25-move level goal. Per-run consumable tools show remaining uses. No game forces a timer. Difficulty parameters cap for playability while new puzzle levels and endless play continue.

Gentle synthesized sound effects cover all nine games, begin on by default after the first player gesture, and can be switched off in Settings or pause. The sound and decorative-motion preferences save on device; reduced-motion also suppresses decoration while essential gameplay continues. Asset loading reports actual progress and provides cancel/retry. Save failure reports feedback without blocking play.

## Brand Commitments

Supplied PH Arcade references establish the white geometric wordmark, official Peak Haven arrow/molecule symbol, happy silver-capped glass vial mascot, alpine bottle laboratories, short collectible glass vials and silver/blue/gold reward chests. The brand palette remains #0A1B3D, #0EA5FF, #38BDF8, #E6F4FF, #FFD24D and #F8FAFC. Extended geometric display uses locally hosted Anybody as the established substitute; practical text uses Inter.

The implemented Alpine Lab direction expresses menus as physical chrome/cobalt consoles, cyan bottle pedestals, gold reward trays, an archive rack and a player terminal. Every route has a distinct illustrated environment. Central sharp portrait art scrolls with its furniture and live menu content; peripheral blurred scenery and a readability tint remain fixed. No opaque white page sheet or outer phone frame. All text, controls, game state and focus remain separate from generated art.

## Product Principles

Mobile first, large action targets, no forced timer, short loops, satisfying material feedback and truthful device-local progress. Keep the inset five-tab navy navigation, proportional art and readable navy backing for information. Reward state and ownership must come from saved data. Never imply store benefits, real payments or customer-account integration.

## Current Menu Experience

Home has a separate mascot illustration, category filtering and a three-column grid containing all nine game objects plus daily rewards. Each game object launches its own game. A live daily merge challenge and Continue a puzzle terminal follow. The game chooser uses two-column pedestal displays with current puzzle level or personal best. Arcade has Sort, Mahjong and Sequence tabs, circular unlimited level nodes, a selected-level launch action and six endless displays.

Rewards makes the daily chest the main gold-tray action, followed by XP, streak, calendar, achievements and coin boost. Bottles displays the equipped vial and a six-bottle archive; checks, unlock arrows and locks distinguish ownership and availability. Profile contains the player terminal, actual stats, bottle rack, achievement tray and settings actions; Settings, Customize and Help share a framed quick-action console. It has no Your Games shelf. At narrow widths the player name wraps across a full second row.

Gameplay combines a real board, glossy three-metric HUD, mounted control dock and a distinct scene per game. Lab Sort composites live cylindrical liquid below transparent chrome glass. Merge keeps falling pieces inside a transparent raster chamber. Tower uses individually bounded sprites, coordinated stack spacing and a moving stack camera. Generated backgrounds and furniture contain no baked labels, controls, scores or board state; each generated asset keeps provenance alongside the shipping file. Orbit, Ridge Run, Bond and Sequence have original, distinct Alpine Lab environments with live canvas gameplay layered above them.
